Research & Methodology

Exploring combustion in microgravity through scientific literature, experimental observations, data analysis and educational visualization.

🌌

Research Problem

Fire behaves differently in microgravity because buoyancy-driven convection is strongly reduced. This changes heat and mass transfer, flame geometry and the movement of combustion products. Understanding these effects is important for spacecraft fire safety.

🎯

Research Goal

To investigate how gravity, oxygen concentration, airflow and material properties influence combustion behavior, using scientific evidence and an interactive educational simulator.

💡

Research Hypothesis

Reduced buoyancy in microgravity can produce more spherical diffusion flames under suitable conditions. Oxygen availability, ventilation and fuel properties may significantly change flame behavior and fire hazards.

📋

Research Objectives

Review NASA combustion experiments; study the influence of gravity, oxygen and airflow; examine available gas measurements; compare material behavior; and communicate scientific concepts through interactive visualization.

🔬

Research Methodology

The project follows a structured research workflow combining literature review, preliminary data exploration and simulation.

01 — Literature Review

Study scientific publications and NASA experiment descriptions related to microgravity combustion and fire safety.

02 — Data Preparation

Organize available experimental records, identify variables, missing values and potential data quality issues.

03 — Exploratory Analysis

Visualize initial and final gas measurements and compare observations without assuming causal relationships.

04 — Educational Simulation

Develop a qualitative visualization of flame behavior under selected gravity, oxygen, ventilation and material conditions.

05 — Interpretation

Compare general simulation behavior with established scientific principles and explain uncertainties and limitations.

🔥

NASA FLEX Experiment

The Flame Extinguishment Experiment investigates combustion of liquid fuel droplets in microgravity, including flame extinction and combustion behavior under conditions where buoyancy effects are reduced.

🧪

NASA BASS and BASS-II

Burning and Suppression of Solids experiments investigate flame spread over solid materials in microgravity, including the influence of ventilation and material characteristics.

📊

Experimental Data

The Data & Analysis section contains a preliminary secondary dataset of 20 BASS-II records with oxygen, carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide measurements. These records require verification against original NASA data.

Interactive Simulation

The simulator illustrates possible qualitative changes in flame shape when gravity, oxygen, ventilation or material selection changes. It is not a validated physical combustion model.

⚖️

Research Limitations

Available numerical records come from a secondary dataset and have not been independently verified against NASA archives. The simulator uses conceptual relationships rather than calibrated combustion equations. No experimental validation, uncertainty quantification or predictive fire-risk assessment has been completed.

Scientific conclusions must distinguish documented NASA findings, preliminary observations and educational illustrations.
